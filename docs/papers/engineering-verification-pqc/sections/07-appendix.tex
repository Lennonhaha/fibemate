Both repositories are public. The commit column records the snapshot at
submission; the commits measured on 2026-10-30 and reported in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} are named in the provenance note there. The corpus
counts in that section are measured at \texttt{b2910d5} on clean checkouts; the
CBOM figures are measured at \texttt{95056b4}.

The formalization repository is deliberately
separate from the product repository: research-line proofs do not ship in the
product, and keeping them in a separate repository makes it impossible for the
two to drift into each other by accident. It also means the proof corpus has its
own remote, which it did not have for the first two days of its existence.

The CBOM schema is vendored in three files under \texttt{tools/schemas/},
because the BOM schema alone is not self-contained: it references the SPDX
licence-expression schema and the JSON Signature Format schema, and loading it
alone makes the validator fail at schema-compile time rather than at validation
time. The recorded hashes are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrl@{}}
\toprule
File & Bytes & SHA-256 (prefix) \\
\midrule
\texttt{bom-1.6.schema.json} & 262{,}666 & \texttt{18f57f7482593bad} \\
\texttt{spdx.schema.json} & 63{,}418 & \texttt{4b345e2329f209f3} \\
\texttt{jsf-0.82.schema.json} & 8{,}058 & \texttt{8bae002c25e723db} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These are the full-file hashes truncated for display; the full values are in the
schemas README. One honest caveat about their provenance: they were
cross-checked against the copies in a sibling tools repository and found
identical, but a direct fetch from the upstream site could not be completed from
the build machine, so what is asserted is ``the vendored copy is identical to
the tools-repository fixture'', not ``re-verified against upstream on the date of
writing''.

\subsection{Recomputing the numbers}

Every count in this paper is produced by a script shipped with the artefact.
None is maintained by hand.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.59\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Figure & Command \\
\midrule
Lemma / theorem / \texttt{sorry} counts & \texttt{node tools/count-obligations.cjs}\newline\textit{[repo: vwz-formalization]} \\
CBOM regeneration & \texttt{node scripts/build-cbom.js}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate]} \\
CBOM staleness check & \texttt{node scripts/build-cbom.js -{}-check}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate]} \\
CBOM schema validation & \texttt{node tools/validate-cbom.cjs}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate]} \\
Corpus claim counts & \texttt{node verifact/bin/verifact.js verify}\newline\texttt{--config verifact/verifact.json}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate-tools]} \\
Disturbance matrix & \texttt{node scripts/claim-evidence/run-e2e.cjs}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate-tools]} \\
Delivery gate & \texttt{node scripts/check-live.cjs}\newline\textit{[repo: fibemate]} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The obligation counter exits non-zero if a \texttt{sorry} appears anywhere it is
not expected. This is deliberate: the counter is the paper's primary claim, so it
is written to fail loudly rather than to report a number quietly.

\subsection{Building the formalization}

\begin{lstlisting}
isabelle build -D aligned    # the main session; see the quick_and_dirty caveat
isabelle build -D smoke      # a small session that must build clean
isabelle build -D neg        # negative control; MUST fail with exit 1
\end{lstlisting}

The third command failing is a prerequisite for the first command meaning
anything. On Windows, \texttt{bin/isabelle} is a shell script and must be invoked
through the bundled Cygwin bash; the repository README records the exact
invocation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Tier & Lemmas & \texttt{sorry} \\
\midrule
T0 --- purely formal rewriting & 17 & 0 \\
T1-A --- algebraic axioms & 16 & 0 \\

\midrule
Total & 56 & 17 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Repository totals: 185 lemmas, 1 theorem and 17 \texttt{sorry}, across 10 theory
files totalling 125{,}165 bytes. The ledger accounts for every \texttt{sorry}
in the repository; the counter reports zero outside it, so the agreement is
enforced rather than merely observed. The comment block at the head of the
ledger still lists the counts from before \texttt{T2\_B2} was closed; the
script's output, not that comment, is the authoritative count.
