\subsection{Repositories and versions}

The snapshot described in this paper is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Artefact & Location & Commit \\
\midrule
Implementation + CBOM & \texttt{github.com/Lennonhaha/fibemate} & \texttt{95056b4} \\
Formalization corpus & \texttt{github.com/Lennonhaha/fibemate-vwz} & \texttt{1361dbb} \\
Isabelle version & Isabelle2025-2 & --- \\
CBOM schema & CycloneDX 1.6, vendored & see below \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both repositories are public. The formalization repository is deliberately
separate from the product repository: research-line proofs do not ship in the
product, and keeping them in a separate repository makes it impossible for the
two to drift into each other by accident. It also means the proof corpus has its
own remote, which it did not have for the first two days of its existence.

The CBOM schema is vendored at \texttt{tools/schemas/} in three files, because
\texttt{bom-1.6.schema.json} is not self-contained: it references
\texttt{spdx.schema.json} and \texttt{jsf-0.82.schema.json}, and loading it alone
makes the validator fail at schema-compile time rather than at validation time.
The recorded hashes are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrl@{}}
\toprule
File & Bytes & SHA-256 (prefix) \\
\midrule
\texttt{bom-1.6.schema.json} & 262{,}666 & \texttt{18f57f7482593bad} \\
\texttt{spdx.schema.json} & 63{,}418 & \texttt{4b345e2329f209f3} \\
\texttt{jsf-0.82.schema.json} & 8{,}058 & \texttt{8bae002c25e723db} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These are the full-file hashes truncated for display; the full values are in
\texttt{tools/schemas/README.md}. One honest caveat about their provenance: they
were cross-checked against the copies in a sibling tools repository and found
identical, but a direct fetch from \texttt{cyclonedx.org} could not be completed
from the build machine, so what is asserted is ``the vendored copy is identical
to the tools-repository fixture'', not ``re-verified against upstream on the date
of writing''.

\subsection{Recomputing the numbers}

Every count in this paper is produced by a script shipped with the artefact.
None is maintained by hand.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Figure & Command \\
\midrule
Lemma / theorem / \texttt{sorry} counts & \texttt{node tools/count-obligations.cjs} \\
CBOM regeneration & \texttt{node scripts/build-cbom.js} \\
CBOM staleness check & \texttt{node scripts/build-cbom.js --check} \\
CBOM schema validation & \texttt{node scripts/validate-cbom.cjs} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The obligation counter exits non-zero if a \texttt{sorry} appears anywhere it is
not expected. This is deliberate: the counter is the paper's primary claim, so it
is written to fail loudly rather than to report a number quietly.

\subsection{Building the formalization}

\begin{lstlisting}
isabelle build -D aligned    # the main session; see the quick_and_dirty caveat
isabelle build -D smoke      # a small session that must build clean
isabelle build -D neg        # negative control; MUST fail with exit 1
\end{lstlisting}

The third command failing is a prerequisite for the first command meaning
anything. On Windows, \texttt{bin/isabelle} is a shell script and must be invoked
through the bundled Cygwin bash; the repository README records the exact
invocation.
